# Hero interaction ideas — September 6

The user likes the accepted coded page and asks to ideate 2–3 original ways to bring the hero margins alive. References: Floor796's miniature world and Unfair's cursor-following eyes. Concepts only: do not build or generate the graphic treatment yet.

Floor796 was visually inspected this turn: dense isometric rooms, independent little scenes, odd characters and discoverable details. The useful principle for this hero is an apparently living world with small discoveries; its full visual density is inappropriate around the headline. Unfair's eye interaction adds a sense of being noticed with little reading burden.

## 1. A tiny internet

Replace the floating quote cards with two or three miniature pixel-art scenes at the margins. They are odd little corners of an online world: a sleepy search kiosk, a strange room where a message arrives, a tiny figure noticing a signal. The center remains quiet.

Interaction: the scene nearest the cursor notices it—a light switches on or a tiny figure looks up. Most of the time the scenes are almost still. A rare small detail rewards looking longer.

Feeling: curious, internet-native, slightly absurd. Strength: the richest long-term identity and the closest connection to Floor796. Tradeoff: requires actual art direction and bespoke sprites; easily becomes too busy. Borrow the living-scene principle, not characters or assets from Floor796.

## 2. The curious infinite

One original small character based on a continuous infinity-shaped silhouette. It belongs in a hero corner or peeks from the dashboard edge, not repeated on every element.

Interaction: notices the cursor, leans toward it, pauses before following and occasionally blinks or ducks away. The pose and timing make it distinctive beyond a pair of cursor-tracking pupils.

Feeling: mischievous, attentive, companion-like without looking like a generic robot. Strength: an ownable recognizable character that can recur in loading, onboarding and small brand moments. Tradeoff: can become cute or gimmicky; character design matters. Avoid Unfair's literal googly-eyed portrait treatment.

## 3. A field of little signals

Sparse tiny ink-blue marks or pixel fragments live at the hero edges. They have a shared visual grammar and leave the copy unobstructed.

Interaction: nearby marks orient toward the cursor; two or three briefly connect as if a signal has found its destination, then settle. When idle, a few loosely form an infinity loop. No points, game, clicking requirement or extra copy.

Feeling: quietly alive, magnetic, precise. Strength: the simplest and lightest implementation. Tradeoff: less character than the first two, and can become a generic particle background unless tightly art-directed.

## Shared constraints

- Replace the existing decorative side cards; do not add another visual layer on top.
- Protect headline and CTA space; responsive layout must not hide or intercept controls.
- No continuous high-energy motion, cursor hijacking, autoplay audio or mandatory interaction.
- Mobile and reduced-motion states must look intentionally composed without tracking behavior.
- Choose the overall feeling before polishing a full implementation.

## Decision

Suggested next brief: “Prototype [tiny internet / curious infinite / signal field] in the existing hero. Keep [qualities], avoid [qualities]. Start with one representative element, not a whole world.”

Recommendation: test the curious infinite first for the smallest ownable signature; choose tiny internet if discovery and world-building matter more. Do not combine all three.
